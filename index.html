The Risk

Manual Logs Leave Dangerous Gaps

Most pharmacies work hard to maintain vaccine cold chain records. The problem is that manual checks and basic loggers often fail at the exact time you need them most — overnight, on weekends, during public holidays, or when no one is watching the fridge.

The Weekend Gap

A manual logbook may show a Friday afternoon reading and a Monday morning reading, but it cannot prove what happened during the 60+ hours in between.

No Alert, No Action

If a fridge compressor fails at 2am, a paper logbook cannot notify anyone. By the time staff arrive, vaccines may already need to be quarantined and assessed.

Weak Audit Evidence

Temperature data without calibration records, alert history, and response evidence may not give you the documentation needed during an audit, accreditation review, or insurance question.

Why It Matters

Cold Chain Compliance Is an Operational Risk, Not an Admin Task

Australian vaccine cold chain guidance is built around keeping vaccines within the recommended +2°C to +8°C range, with +5°C as the target midpoint under "Strive for 5". Sentry Compliance is designed around the +2°C to +8°C vaccine cold chain range and supports National Vaccine Storage Guidelines 'Strive for 5' requirements. When monitoring is manual, disconnected, or uncalibrated, the risk is not just product loss — it is incomplete evidence.
